# Sitewide search metadata — 18 November 2026

The supplied Search Console export covers 16 August–15 November 2026. All 527 URL rows were reviewed. The selected set contains 174 non-fragment URLs with at least 50 impressions and CTR below 1%: 140 Vietnamese and 34 English pages. Eighteen selected pages rank below position 20; they remain in scope at the owner's request. These thresholds select an experiment, not an industry CTR benchmark.

All 174 selected pages receive separately edited titles and descriptions grounded in their current article headings. Other pages lose trailing AC/Apocalypse Closet title branding; the root homepage keeps its full brand suffix. Existing Open Graph and Twitter text is synchronized; selected pages get missing fields where needed. Article/WebPage schema text and the current-page breadcrumb label follow the updated metadata. Organization, product, image, FAQ and other unrelated schema values are preserved.

Changes are restricted to title, description/social metadata and the corresponding schema text. Some legacy documents place metadata after the closing head; those existing tags are updated in place without restructuring the document. All non-metadata markup, H1s, prose, links, images, canonical and hreflang values remain exactly unchanged. The release manifest pins the master predecessor and before/after hashes. Current metadata QA inspects actual successors and independently verifies the allowed edit surface. Existing image and editorial preservation gates only receive exact verified predecessor bytes; their current semantic and link checks still inspect the actual current pages.

The export contains separate query and page aggregates, so it cannot prove query-to-page associations or identify why a page has low CTR. The English and Vietnamese experiments retain their own languages. Google may rewrite title links or snippets, and recrawl timing varies. The whole-site before/after change does not establish causation; compare CTR, clicks, impressions and position within matching query/country/device groups after recrawl, initially over 28 days and longer for sparse pages. Keep a dated record of other deployments. US conversion outcomes need separate measurement from predominantly Vietnamese clicks.

The original evidence and metadata records are in `data/search-metadata-2026-11-18/changes.csv` and `proposals.json`. No ranking or click increase is promised. Meta descriptions describe content and avoid invented prices, dates or availability claims.
